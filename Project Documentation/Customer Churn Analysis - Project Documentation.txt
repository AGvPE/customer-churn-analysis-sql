CUSTOMER CHURN ANALYSIS USING SQL

Prepared by: Agape Watat
Tools Used: SQL, SQLite, DB Browser for SQLite
Dataset: IBM Telco Customer Churn Dataset


1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

Customer churn occurs when customers discontinue their relationship with a business or stop using its products or services.

This project analyses customer churn data from a telecommunications company to understand customer behaviour, identify patterns associated with customer attrition, and highlight customer segments with higher churn rates.

Using SQL, the analysis examines customer demographics, contract types, payment methods, internet services, subscription features, customer tenure, and billing information.

The objective is to transform raw customer data into meaningful business insights that can help the company understand its customer retention challenges and develop more effective retention strategies.

2. BUSINESS PROBLEM

Customer churn can negatively affect a telecommunications company's revenue, customer relationships, and long-term business growth.

The company needs to understand which customers are more likely to discontinue its services and what customer characteristics or service-related factors are associated with higher churn rates.

Without proper analysis, it may be difficult to identify high-risk customer segments and develop targeted customer retention strategies.

This project addresses that challenge by analysing customer data to uncover churn patterns and provide insights that can support better business decisions.


3. PROJECT OBJECTIVES

The key objectives of this analysis are to:

i. Determine the overall customer churn and retention rates.

ii. Identify contract types with the highest customer churn rates.

iii. Examine churn patterns across different payment methods and internet service categories.

iv. Analyse the relationship between customer tenure and churn.

v. Compare churn rates based on customer demographics and subscription services.

vi. Examine average monthly and total charges across churn categories.

vii. Identify customer segments that may require targeted retention strategies.

viii. Provide data-driven recommendations to support customer retention efforts.


4. DATASET DESCRIPTION

The analysis uses the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset, which contains customer information from a telecommunications company.

The dataset consists of 7,043 customer records and includes information on:

- Customer demographics: Gender, senior citizen status, and dependants.
- Account information: Tenure, contract type, and payment method.
- Services subscribed to: Internet service, online security, and technical support.
- Billing information: Monthly charges and total charges.
- Customer status: Whether a customer churned or remained with the company.

The dataset provides the information needed to examine customer behaviour and identify patterns associated with customer churn.

6. ANALYSIS APPROACH

The analysis was conducted using SQL queries in SQLite. The process involved the following stages:

6.1 Data Overview and Validation

- Examined the total number of customer records.
- Identified the number of customers who had discontinued their services.
- Established the dataset size before proceeding with further analysis.

6.2 Overall Churn Analysis

- Calculated the total number of customers who churned and those who remained.
- Determined the overall churn rate and customer retention rate.

6.3 Customer Segmentation Analysis

- Analysed churn across different contract types.
- Compared churn rates across payment methods.
- Grouped customers by tenure to examine how churn varied over time.
- Examined churn patterns across internet service categories.
- Compared churn between senior and non-senior customers.

6.4 Billing Analysis

- Compared average monthly charges between churned and retained customers.
- Compared average total charges across both customer groups.
- Examined average monthly charges across contract types and churn status.

6.5 Service Subscription Analysis

- Examined churn among customers with and without technical support.
- Analysed churn across online security subscription categories.
- Combined technical support and online security subscriptions to identify differences in churn rates.

6.6 Combined Customer Segmentation

- Combined contract type and internet service to identify customer groups with particularly high or low churn rates.

SQL functions and techniques used included:
- COUNT() and SUM()
- AVG()
- ROUND()
- CASE WHEN statements
- GROUP BY
- ORDER BY
- WHERE
- Aggregate calculations and conditional logic.

7. KEY FINDINGS

The analysis revealed several important patterns in customer churn.

7.1 Overall Customer Churn

- Total customers analysed: 7,043.
- Customers who churned: 1,869.
- Customers retained: 5,174.
- Overall churn rate: 26.54%.
- Customer retention rate: 73.46%.

Approximately 27 out of every 100 customers in the dataset had discontinued their services.

7.2 Churn by Contract Type

- Month-to-month contracts recorded the highest churn rate at 42.71%.
- One-year contracts recorded a churn rate of 11.27%.
- Two-year contracts recorded the lowest churn rate at 2.83%.

Customers on month-to-month contracts showed substantially higher churn rates than customers on longer-term contracts.

7.3 Churn by Payment Method

- Electronic check users had the highest churn rate at 45.29%.
- Mailed check users recorded 19.11%.
- Bank transfer users recorded 16.71%.
- Credit card users recorded the lowest churn rate at 15.24%.

Electronic check users represented a particularly high-churn customer segment.

7.4 Churn by Customer Tenure

- Customers with 0–12 months of tenure had the highest churn rate at 47.44%.
- Customers with 13–24 months recorded 28.71%.
- Customers with 25–48 months recorded 20.39%.
- Customers with 49–60 months recorded 14.42%.
- Customers with 61–72 months recorded the lowest churn rate at 6.61%.

The results show a clear pattern: churn rates decreased as customer tenure increased.

7.5 Churn by Internet Service

- Fiber optic customers recorded the highest churn rate at 41.89%.
- DSL customers recorded 18.96%.
- Customers without internet service recorded the lowest rate at 7.40%.

Fiber optic customers represented a high-churn segment requiring further investigation.

7.6 Churn by Senior Citizen Status

- Senior citizens recorded a churn rate of 41.68%.
- Non-senior customers recorded a churn rate of 23.61%.

The senior customer segment had a higher churn rate than non-senior customers.

7.7 Average Charges by Churn Status

- Customers who churned had average monthly charges of 74.44.
- Retained customers had average monthly charges of 61.27.
- Retained customers had higher average total charges of 2,555.34 compared with 1,531.80 for churned customers.

The higher average total charges among retained customers may be associated with their longer customer tenure.

7.8 Churn by Technical Support

- Customers without technical support recorded a churn rate of 41.64%.
- Customers with technical support recorded 15.17%.
- Customers without internet service recorded 7.40%.

Customers without technical support had a substantially higher churn rate.

7.9 Churn by Online Security

- Customers without online security recorded a churn rate of 41.77%.
- Customers with online security recorded 14.61%.
- Customers without internet service recorded 7.40%.

Customers without online security showed a substantially higher churn rate.

7.10 Churn by Combined Support and Security Services

Among customers with internet service:

- Customers with neither technical support nor online security recorded a churn rate of 48.96%.
- Customers with only one of the two services recorded 21.82%.
- Customers with both services recorded the lowest churn rate of 9.01%.

This suggests a strong association between subscription to these additional services and lower customer churn.

7.11 Churn by Contract Type and Internet Service

- Month-to-month Fiber optic customers recorded the highest churn rate at 54.61%.
- Month-to-month DSL customers recorded 32.22%.
- Two-year Fiber optic customers recorded 7.23%.
- Two-year DSL customers recorded the lowest churn rate at 1.91%.

The combination of month-to-month contracts and Fiber optic internet service represented the highest-risk segment among the combinations analysed.

7.12 Average Monthly Charges by Contract and Churn Status

Customers who churned had higher average monthly charges than retained customers across all three contract types:

- Month-to-month: 73.02 compared with 61.46.
- One-year: 85.05 compared with 62.51.
- Two-year: 86.78 compared with 60.01.

This indicates that higher average monthly charges were associated with churn across the contract categories.

8. BUSINESS RECOMMENDATIONS

Based on the findings, the following actions are recommended to help improve customer retention:

8.1 Strengthen Customer Retention During the First Year

Since customers within their first 12 months recorded the highest churn rate (47.44%), the company should introduce an early-stage retention programme.

Possible actions include:
- Improving customer onboarding.
- Conducting follow-up communication with new customers.
- Providing early customer support and engagement.
- Identifying dissatisfaction before customers decide to leave.

8.2 Encourage Longer-Term Contract Adoption

Month-to-month customers recorded a significantly higher churn rate than customers on one-year and two-year contracts.

The company could:
- Introduce incentives for customers who switch to longer-term contracts.
- Offer loyalty benefits.
- Communicate the value of longer-term subscriptions.

8.3 Investigate High Churn Among Fiber Optic Customers

Fiber optic customers recorded a churn rate of 41.89%, with month-to-month Fiber optic customers reaching 54.61%.

The company should investigate:
- Service reliability and internet performance.
- Customer complaints and service interruptions.
- Pricing competitiveness.
- Customer satisfaction with the Fiber optic service.

8.4 Review the Electronic Check Payment Experience

Electronic check users recorded the highest payment-method churn rate at 45.29%.

The company should investigate whether payment convenience, failed transactions, or payment-related customer experiences contribute to dissatisfaction.

Alternative payment methods could also be promoted where appropriate.

8.5 Promote Technical Support and Online Security Services

Customers without technical support or online security recorded substantially higher churn rates.

The company could:
- Improve awareness of these services.
- Offer relevant subscription bundles.
- Communicate the benefits of additional security and support services.
- Evaluate whether these services improve customer satisfaction and retention.

8.6 Review Pricing and Customer Value

Customers who churned had higher average monthly charges than retained customers.

The company should investigate pricing concerns, perceived service value, and affordability, particularly among customers on longer-term contracts who churned.

8.7 Introduce Targeted Retention Strategies

Rather than applying the same retention strategy to every customer, the company should prioritise segments with higher observed churn rates.

These include:
- Customers within their first 12 months.
- Month-to-month Fiber optic customers.
- Electronic check users.
- Customers without technical support or online security.
- Senior citizen customers.

The effectiveness of these recommendations should be monitored using customer retention and churn metrics over time.

9. PROJECT CONCLUSION

This project analysed 7,043 telecommunications customer records using SQL to identify patterns associated with customer churn.

The analysis revealed an overall churn rate of 26.54% and highlighted several customer segments with notably higher churn rates.

Key observations included:

- Customers on month-to-month contracts had substantially higher churn rates than those on longer-term contracts.
- Customers within their first 12 months had the highest churn rate.
- Fiber optic customers and electronic check users showed relatively high churn rates.
- Customers without technical support or online security recorded higher churn rates.
- Customers who churned had higher average monthly charges than retained customers.

The findings provide useful insights into customer behaviour and highlight areas where the company could investigate and improve its retention strategies.

Through this project, SQL was used to transform raw customer records into structured analysis and actionable business insights.


10. PROJECT LIMITATIONS

Although the analysis provides useful insights, certain limitations should be considered:

1. The analysis identifies relationships and patterns but does not establish cause-and-effect relationships.

2. The dataset represents a specific telecommunications customer population, so the findings may not apply to every telecommunications company.

3. The analysis uses the variables available in the dataset. Other factors, such as customer satisfaction scores, complaint history, competitor pricing, and service interruption records, were not included.

4. The analysis is primarily descriptive and does not use predictive modelling to estimate the likelihood of individual customers churning.

5. The dataset does not establish a specific reporting period for the overall churn rate; therefore, the 26.54% churn rate should not be interpreted as a monthly or annual rate.
